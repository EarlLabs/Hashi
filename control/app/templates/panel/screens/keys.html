{# Keys & configs — what each node holds, and how to replace it. design/README.md §7 asks for scope, transport, fingerprint (truncated, mono, muted), minted, rotates and a "rotate" action, over a lead paragraph stating the rule. The columns here are that list with two honest substitutions, both recorded rather than quietly made: * `minted` became `on the node`. Nothing in this schema records WHEN a piece of material was minted — a row's timestamps move when anything on it changes, so a cell labelled "minted" would be a date that is right until the first rotation and silently wrong afterwards. What the control plane genuinely holds is when a configuration carrying this material last reached the host, which is also the fact a rotation's failure mode is about. * `rotates` is gone. It asks for a schedule, and there is none: nothing in this product rotates anything on its own, and a column promising otherwise would be the panel telling the operator a job is being done for them. Its space went to `users on it`, which is the blast radius of the button in the same row. THE LEAD PARAGRAPH IS THE SCREEN'S POINT, not decoration. UI-PATTERNS §9: where key material could appear, the panel prints the rule instead — so this screen, whose whole subject is material that may never be displayed, states the rule once at the top and then keeps it in every cell. Nothing on it is derived from a private or pre-shared value: a fingerprint is a short hash of the PUBLISHED half a transport declares (see services/keymaterial.py, which is handed the clear parameters and never the encrypted ones), and a transport with no published half gets the rule in that cell instead of a value. #} {% include "panel/console_header.html" %}

Key material is minted by the control plane, stored encrypted, and never displayed again — not here, not in a dialog, not on any other screen. A fingerprint is a short hash of the half that is already published to the devices that use it, so nothing on this screen can be turned back into a key. You never type one in either: the only credential this panel accepts is the SSH key on the add-node dialog, which is stored encrypted the moment it arrives and never rendered again.
